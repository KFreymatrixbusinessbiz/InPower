# InPower Doctrine

This document is not marketing.

It is not copywriting.

It is not a manifesto.

It is a collection of observations about organizations that remain true whether InPower exists or not.

Every future page, presentation, proposal, article, keynote, and conversation must align with these principles.

Nothing is added unless it survives repeated scrutiny.

Nothing remains because it sounds impressive.

Only observations that become increasingly true over time belong here.

---

## 01

Every organization is designed around assumptions.

---

## 02

Assumptions shape operating models.

---

## 03

Operating models create dependencies.

---

## 04

The most consequential dependencies become ordinary.

---

## 05

What becomes ordinary is rarely questioned.

---

## 06

Organizations rarely choose dependency.

They inherit it.

---

## 07

Technology rarely creates the operating model.

It inherits it.

---

## 08

Operational dependency is not inevitable.

It is designed.

---

## 09

If dependency can be designed...

it can be redesigned.

---

## 10

Most organizations optimize around assumptions they have never challenged.

---

## 11

Changing technology is easy.

Changing assumptions is difficult.

---

## 12

The operating model determines the outcome long before technology does.

---

## 13

Recovery begins where dependency ends.

---

## 14

Outside dependency reduces operational control.

Inside capability creates operational continuity.

---

## 15

Print is simply where we chose to begin.

---

## Editorial Standard

Every statement must satisfy four tests.

1. Would this remain true ten years from now?

2. Would this remain true if InPower did not exist?

3. Would an executive recognize this as a truth about their own organization?

4. Does this describe reality instead of promoting a product?

If the answer is no...

it does not belong here.

---

## Final Principle

Do not persuade.

Reveal.

The strongest ideas do not force agreement.

They become difficult to disagree with.
