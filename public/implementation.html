What changed

Work changed. Output changed. Technology changed.

Information increasingly moves to distributed locations before physical output is produced. Technology can also remove process complexity and reduce reasons for conventional mechanical intervention. InPower asks whether the support model should change with them.

What should the organization be able to control?

  • Workflows, locations and where output is needed
  • The current fleet and its dependencies
  • Continuity, security and operating requirements
  • Existing internal capability and resources
  • The organization's preferred operating philosophy

The operating model is evaluated with the customer—not imposed on the customer.

  • Match technology to workflow and location requirements
  • Recognize existing aptitude, competence and resources
  • Establish the capability the organization wants to own
  • Define appropriate first response and deeper technical capability
  • Preserve the external support and specialized expertise that should remain

After

Operate with control and choice.

Capability moves closer. Support does not disappear.

  • Appropriate first response can begin where the interruption occurs
  • Deeper internal capability may exist where the organization is equipped for it
  • Applicable platform and manufacturer resources remain available
  • Matrix remains an experienced resource and backstop when requested
  • Specialized conditions continue through the appropriate support path

The recovery boundary

The customer is not becoming the technician.

Handle what the organization is equipped and authorized to handle. Escalate what requires something more. The boundary varies with the platform, organizational capability, available resources, security and warranty requirements, operating environment, and customer preference.

Appropriate capability

Clear first-response actions and, where suitable, deeper bounded capability for designated technical personnel.

Specialized intervention

Conditions requiring advanced diagnosis, restricted procedures, specialized tooling, manufacturer resources, parts, replacement, or external service.

Matrix's role

Visible in design. Available in operation.

Matrix helps understand the environment, evaluate fit, recommend an operating architecture, and establish an appropriate recovery model. During normal operation, Matrix remains available for practical intelligence and support without becoming the required first call for every interruption.

Questions before adoption

What the model does—and does not—require.

Does InPower mean our employees become copier technicians?

No. Ordinary users perform only clear, appropriate first-response actions. Deeper internal capability is optional and depends on the platform, personnel, resources and defined recovery boundary. Specialized expertise remains specialized.

Does InPower eliminate service?

No. It reduces unnecessary dependency on external intervention where technology and organizational capability make another response appropriate. Support remains available beyond the internal boundary.

Does every organization handle the same things internally?

No. Capability varies with the platform, environment, personnel, available resources and the organization's preferred operating model.

Does Matrix have to be our first call?

Not universally. When available internal capability is exceeded, the organization follows the appropriate support path for the platform and condition. Matrix remains available as an experienced resource and backstop.

Is InPower self-service?

No. It is governed capability: appropriate people use appropriate resources within a defined boundary, while external support and specialized intervention remain available.
