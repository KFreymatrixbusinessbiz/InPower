Dependency

The problem isn’t service. The problem is avoidable operational dependency.

External expertise remains necessary and valuable. Dependency becomes avoidable when every recovery must wait for that expertise—even when an appropriate condition can be handled safely by the people already supporting the operation.

NECESSARY EXTERNAL EXPERTISE

Keep specialized work specialized.

Do not export every first response.

What changes

Modern print should improve the operation around it.

OUTCOME 01

Opportunity to lower operating costs

Reduce avoidable travel, labor, parts replacement, inventory, and service overhead where the selected architecture and operating conditions allow.

OUTCOME 02

Potentially fewer service calls

Use engineering that removes many of the wear components that historically created service events, reducing common causes of service without implying every event can be eliminated.

OUTCOME 03

A tighter access boundary

Reduce unnecessary third-party access by keeping appropriate recovery knowledge inside the organization while preserving controlled specialist access when required.

OUTCOME 04

Faster recovery for appropriate conditions

Resolve many common interruptions directly at the device through trained, guided recovery instead of waiting for a technician.

OUTCOME 05

Less routine burden on IT

Designed for the people already supporting operations. Your employees gain safe recovery capability; they do not become copier technicians.

OUTCOME 06

Remote and secure operations

Support locations where technician response is expensive, delayed, restricted, or unavailable.

INPOWER®

The operating model

Modern engineering made a different way to operate possible.

InPower combines resilient print technology, structured technical training, guided recovery, and controlled escalation into a 24/7 operating model. Common recovery moves inside; specialized service remains available when the condition genuinely requires it.

Engineering

Technology selected for the operational outcome it creates.

Capability

Knowledge transferred to the people already supporting the operation.

Continuity

Recovery begins inside the organization, with escalation only when needed.

Technology fit

The model stays consistent. The recovery path changes by platform.

InPower is not a manufacturer program. Matrix selects the architecture that best supports the operating requirement, then defines the training, recovery boundary, parts strategy, and escalation path for that platform.

DIGITIZED A3

Epson PrecisionCore

Heat-free image creation removes much of the mechanical complexity associated with laser imaging. The architecture supports high-volume operation, panel-led recovery, fewer replacement components, and platform knowledge transfer.

Fit: campuses, secure environments, centralized volume, and operations seeking to reduce on-site service dependency.
RESILIENT A4

Brother Workhorse and Titan

Durable distributed devices support a replacement-based recovery model when on-site repair would add cost or delay. The operating plan emphasizes rapid exchange, simple deployment, and continuity across many locations.

Fit: branches, remote teams, distributed operations, and environments where technician dispatch is inefficient.
MODULAR REQUIREMENTS

Selected Kyocera platforms

Some workflows, media requirements, and finishing environments still call for modular heat-based technology. InPower defines which recovery capability belongs inside and which conditions require specialized escalation.

Fit: requirements where application needs determine the architecture and a controlled service boundary matters.

Modern print economics

When engineering changes, the economics deserve to be reconsidered.

Traditional cost-per-page models were built around technologies that consumed parts, maintenance, and technician labor. Modern architectures create the opportunity for economics that reflect the engineering now in use.
